fix(levels): correct the full_adder and decoder availability notes

Two content reviews of chapter-2 levels 13-27 approved the substance and found
the notes disagreeing with the commit that landed the defs. Six corrections, no
`threeStar` value moved:

* Batch 2's module note said NO DEF OF `full_adder` WAS REGISTERED and handed a
  later task two open decisions; `523a7b9` had already registered it (9 NAND
  equivalents, the shared `FULL_ADDER` basis `wide.ts` prices `add8` with) and
  both decisions are now taken. The note, level 20's comment and the batch
  test's header carry the history plus the live facts, and a test asserts the
  reward id resolves.
* The amended ruling offers `full_adder` at level 20 AND level 22, withholding
  it at 21. Level 22's palette gains the part -- one 1-bit instance cannot
  answer an 8-bit adder, while eight of them and the carry chain between them
  ARE the cascade the level teaches -- and its comment records the drop-in's
  measurement rather than a promise: 72 gates and 8 delay against the
  hand-wired reference's 120 and 17, three stars either way, so the target is
  the reference's own measurement and the comment says outright that it does
  not separate the two constructions. Level 21's comment is rewritten as the
  settled decision (one drop-in is that level's exact I/O, measured at 9 gates
  and 1 delay against the 15-and-3 target), and the clause the reviewer read
  literally -- "the gate target is what denies three stars to a player who
  spends a sixth gate" -- is gone: 5 is a component count, 15 is a
  NAND-equivalent count, and six cheap parts are inside 15 and pass.
* Level 22's palette also gains the seven byte operators levels 18 and 19
  unlocked, so the file's own rule ("everything else on the wide shelf is
  offered") describes the palette the code builds; the rule itself is restated
  in its three real categories, including the shelf that is curated rather than
  "everything the player holds".
* Level 26 had level 25's unrecorded seam: `decoder3` is its own reward AND one
  drop-in answers it -- measured at 27 gates, 1 delay, three stars against the
  27-and-3 target, the registered part's `gateCost` being the very minterm tree
  the level teaches. The batch header now names both levels, level 26's comment
  carries the measurement and the reason the part is kept rather than withdrawn
  (no other level offers `decoder3`, so withdrawal would be dead content), and
  the batch-3 test grades the drop-in and compares it with the target.
* `wide.ts`: the decoder section note recommends `2 ** sel` rather than the
  `1 << sel` its own generator rejects, and the width-5 rationale names the
  32-bit port carrier instead of a false `number`-exactness ceiling; a third
  instance of that same claim at `toUint` is corrected too.
* Level 26's SOURCED clause no longer claims the generator as the source's --
  it moves to AUTHORED, while the catalog's `2-Bit Decoder` sourcing stays in
  level 25 (whose own SOURCED/AUTHORED blur is corrected the same way).

Tests: 913 pass across 23 files -- batch 2 at 59 (registry check plus the
level-21 and level-22 drop-in measurements) and batch 3 at 69 (the level-26
measurement); every `threeStar` still equals its reference's measured metrics
and every counterexample still fails. `tsc --noEmit` and `pnpm build` clean.
